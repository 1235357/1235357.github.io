# A clear route through the portfolio and RAG lab

The homepage serves a reader deciding whether to explore Tony's work. The lab
serves a reader working with a PDF. Both use the same light surfaces, Manrope,
cobalt actions and readable body text. The engineering journal and every article
now use that same light theme, including code, tables, navigation and figures.

## Information before interaction

| Reader's question | Initial view | Available on request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who is this? | Name, Northwestern MSCS, focus and internship availability | Education and academic details |
| What has he built? | Three selected projects, short summaries, current phases and primary links | Individual role, complete contribution records, technology lists and three further projects |
| How do I try the lab? | Local PDF selection, passage preparation, question and result workspace | Model preparation, exact asset contracts and the independent source-cell comparator |
| How can I inspect the evidence? | A clearly named notebook entry | Direct section links for source pages, passages, vectors, retrieval, prompt context and receipts |

Project phases remain visible. Detailed records preserve the original facts;
folding them does not change what is claimed. The same embedded workspace is
retained when expanded, so reading a notebook section does not restart parsing,
download assets or run a model.

The notebook's six section links open the containing disclosure and focus the
requested view. The initial URL fragment and history changes take the same
route. Native anchor navigation remains available, and modified clicks or links
to another document do not change the current notebook. The retained receipt
section follows the inspection views rather than preceding them.

The question panel gives the next preparation action from accepted artifacts:
choose a PDF, build source-linked chunks, embed passages, then ask a question.
This guidance is optional presentation data for compatibility with older
templates. It cannot enable an action or bypass a runtime gate.

## Research and application

- [Yingda Yu's reference homepage](https://yingda-yu.github.io/Dean-Yu-050325.github.io/)
  was inspected in the Codex browser. Its identity, short introduction and clear
  primary links inform the reading hierarchy. Its biographical and research
  claims are not Tony's source material.
- [NN/G: Progressive Disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)
  supports exposing common actions first and giving clearly labeled routes to
  secondary detail. The selected-work summaries and notebook index apply that
  distinction.
- [GOV.UK Design System: Accordion](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/accordion/)
  explains the costs of hiding content. Essential actions, current project
  phases, privacy and download implications stay discoverable; details are not
  a substitute for clear writing.
- [Emil Kowalski's design engineering skill](https://github.com/emilkowalski/skills/blob/main/skills/emil-design-eng/SKILL.md)
  guides consistent control behavior, useful defaults and immediate keyboard
  navigation. Notebook jumps use no decorative scroll animation.
- [Archify](https://github.com/tt-a1i/archify) is used for the existing explorable
  architecture, lifecycle and sequence figures. Their specifications and
  delivery receipts remain alongside the figures. The default figure theme is
  light throughout the site, including standalone figure pages and exports.

Filesystem and automated contract checks establish their named source scope.
Actual Codex browser review separately checks rendering, navigation and exports.
Neither constitutes physical-device, screen-reader, user-aesthetic or financial
answer acceptance.
